# Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena

**Date**: 10-23-2026 • [Homebase](../README.md) • #nlp #llm #evaluation #benchmarks

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4

### TL;DR:   
- Explored the usage of strong LLMs as judges to evaluate models on open-ended questions  
- Results showed that judges like GPT-4 match human preferences (80% agreement) = the same level of agreement as humans

### Problem:   
- Current benchmarks do not account for human preferences  
	- existing evaluations only focus on a confined set of tasks (like multi-choice knowledge and retrieval questions) but they don't assess the ability of the LLM to follow user instructions in multi-turn conversations  
- We need an automated method to evaluate LLM alignment with human preferences


**Problems with their initial LLM approach:** (some solutions provided in [Design choices + why](#design-choices--why))  
- LLM-as-a-judge approach still is susceptible to different biases (position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and limited reasoning ability)  
	- **Position bias:** LLMs prefer answers in the first position over other positions  
	- **Verbosity bias:** LLM judge favors longer, verbose responses (even if they are convoluted or low-quality)  
	- **Self-enhancement bias:** LLM judges like the answers generated by themselves   
	- **Limited capacity in grading math/reasoning questions:** Some LLMs struggle to grade math/reasoning problems because they themselves do not know the correct answer  
		- However, interestingly, even when the LLM was able to independently arrive at the correct answer, it was mislead by the provided answers it was meant to grade

### Design choices + why:   
Proposed 3 LLM-as-a-judge variations  
1. Pairwise comparison  
	1. Judge is presented with a question and 2 answers --> has to determine which one is better/tie  
2. Single answer grading  
	1. LLM asked to assign a score to a single answer  
3. Reference-guided grading  
	1. Providing a reference solution

**Solutions to the biases presented:**  
- Swapping positions of the answer choices (use conservative approach: only consider the grading when the answer chosen is consistent in both orderings)  
- Few-shot judge: giving the judge examples of good judgement  
- Chain-of-thought and reference-guided judges:  
	- CoT: was not super effective  
	- Reference-guided: the LLM first independently solves the problem, then the answer is given to the judge in the prompt to use
